# Exploratory Data Analysis & Visualization Directory

This directory contains the visualization artifacts generated by the EDA pipeline (`notebooks/02_exploratory_data_analysis.ipynb`). Each figure illustrates critical behavioral patterns, distributional properties, and predictive relationships within the Instacart grocery dataset.

---

### 1. `01_target_class_distribution.png`
- **Plot Type**: Barplot (Categorical count distribution)
- **Data Inspected**: `reordered` target variable (0 = first-time trial, 1 = reordered item)
- **What it tells us**:
  - Reorders account for **59.8%** of all order-product transactions, while new item trials account for **40.2%**.
  - Confirms moderate class imbalance. While not extreme, standard classification accuracy will be misleading; models must be evaluated using Precision, Recall, F1-score, and PR-AUC.

---

### 2. `02_orders_by_day_of_week.png`
- **Plot Type**: Barplot (Temporal frequency)
- **Data Inspected**: `order_dow` (Day of week from 0 to 6, where 0=Sunday, 1=Monday)
- **What it tells us**:
  - Transaction volume peaks sharply on Sunday (day 0) and Monday (day 1), followed by a steady drop during mid-week (Tuesday through Thursday).
  - Customers plan meals and perform bulk household restocking during weekends and early week. Temporal encoding (one-hot or cyclical sine/cosine) is vital for predictive models.

---

### 3. `03_orders_by_hour_of_day.png`
- **Plot Type**: Line plot with markers (Diurnal trend)
- **Data Inspected**: `order_hour_of_day` (0 to 23 hours)
- **What it tells us**:
  - Purchasing follows a clear diurnal curve: order activity increases starting at 08:00 AM, plateaus during peak daytime hours (10:00 AM to 04:00 PM), and declines rapidly after 08:00 PM.
  - Night hours (00:00 to 06:00) represent minimal traffic. Binning hours into discrete business segments (morning, peak afternoon, evening, night) provides strong predictive signal.

---

### 4. `04_days_since_prior_order_distribution.png`
- **Plot Type**: Histogram with Kernel Density Estimate (KDE)
- **Data Inspected**: `days_since_prior_order` (0 to 30 days)
- **What it tells us**:
  - Strong recurring spikes occur precisely at **7, 14, 21, and 30 days**, reflecting weekly and monthly replenishment cadences.
  - The distinct spike at day 30 represents provider-level right-censoring/capping for orders placed 30+ days apart. Modulo-7 cyclical features capture these replenishment rhythms.

---

### 5. `05_basket_size_distribution.png`
- **Plot Type**: Histogram with KDE
- **Data Inspected**: Basket size (item count grouped by `order_id`)
- **What it tells us**:
  - Basket size is positively skewed (right-tailed) with a median of 8 items and mode between 5 and 7 items.
  - A small proportion of orders exceed 25–40 items. Log-transformations stabilize variance for parametric models sensitive to heavy tails.

---

### 6. `06_basket_size_boxplot_outliers.png`
- **Plot Type**: Boxplot (Categorical comparison with outlier dispersion)
- **Data Inspected**: `add_to_cart_order` stratified by `reordered` status (0 vs 1)
- **What it tells us**:
  - Reordered products have significantly lower median and upper quartile `add_to_cart_order` positions than non-reordered products.
  - Customers systematically add regular household staples to their carts first, while impulse and experimental items are added later in the session.

---

### 7. `07_top_departments_and_reorder_rate.png`
- **Plot Type**: Horizontal barplot (Category volume and conditional target mean)
- **Data Inspected**: `department` vs mean `reordered`
- **What it tells us**:
  - `produce` and `dairy eggs` constitute the vast majority of item volume and exhibit the highest reorder propensities (>65%).
  - In contrast, pantry staples, personal care, and specialty departments show reorder rates under 35%. Target-encoding or embedding categorical departments provides strong classification leverage.

---

### 8. `08_correlation_heatmap.png`
- **Plot Type**: Annotated heatmap (Pearson correlation matrix)
- **Data Inspected**: `order_number`, `order_dow`, `order_hour_of_day`, `days_since_prior_order`, `add_to_cart_order`, `reordered`
- **What it tells us**:
  - `add_to_cart_order` has a noticeable negative correlation ($r \approx -0.15$) with `reordered`.
  - `order_number` correlates positively ($r \approx +0.12$) with `reordered`, demonstrating that more experienced users exhibit higher repurchase loyalty.
  - Multicollinearity among predictors is low ($|r| < 0.2$), confirming stability for linear, regularized, and tree-based model training.
